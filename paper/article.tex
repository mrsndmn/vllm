\begin{abstract}
This working draft introduces the evaluation workload for experiments on
activation and weight zeroing. We report input and output text lengths from
completed unmodified baseline runs and their repeats. These descriptive
statistics characterize the workload; they do not establish speedups or the
accuracy effects of zeroing. We also report thirty completed evaluations of
joint activation zeroing, joint weight zeroing, and their combination. The
combinations show task-dependent quality changes, including losses on code
generation and reasoning alongside largely preserved long-context retrieval.
Finally, eighteen evaluations with a thinking checkpoint and extended reasoning
budget retain high accuracy under seven selected interventions. We report their
input and full-generation token lengths, and identify increased generation-cap
exhaustion under joint embedding and output-head zeroing.
\end{abstract}
